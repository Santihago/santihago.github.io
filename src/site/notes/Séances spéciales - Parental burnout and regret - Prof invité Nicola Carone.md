## Definitions

*Parental Burnout: What is it, and why does it matter (Mikolajczak, Gross, Roskam)* 2018

Clinical syndrome.

Stress chronique. Degré de stress est normal chronique comme condition.  
Existait assomption implicite que le stress occupational (au travail) provoquait le burnout parental. Mais recherche a montre que faible correlation entre les deux.

Symptoms, core-features:
1. **Overwhelming exhaustion**: related with the parental role, not general fatigue
2. **Saturation**: loss of fulfillment in the parental role
3. **Emotional distancing**: caregiving becomes mechanical (similar with depressed parents)
4. **Contrast with previous self** (parent I am vs parent I want to be or was)

Differential diagnosis:
- **Parenting stress** (transient, no role abandonment or exhaustion)
- **Occupational burnout** (work-specific, does not predict neglect/violence toward children)
- **Depression** (pervasive across all life domains)
- **Postpartum depression** (time-limited, hormonally linked)
- **General exhaustion** (does not include contrast with previous self and emotional distancing)
No cut-off/set duration of symptoms for diagnosis (not disorder recognized in diagnostic manuals, it's a "parental" disorder. Current tools assess for presence over the last x weeks. Little longitudinal evidence).

Question du réseau de soutien, implication de famille et grand-parents dans le caregiving:
- some evidence from cross-cultural research that it's more common in individualistic countries: culture can make a difference (the way parenting is defined..) 
- social support is one of the protective factors, but it's not the only factor
- Parental burnout is related to past childhood experiences
- Question of feeling legitimate to ask for help, why don't some of us ask for that help? Question du style d'attachment (internal working model as self-sufficient)...

**Why parenting is a burnout-prone activity**
Bornstein 2020.
Paradox of parenting, ambivalence of being happy of being a parent (joy, positive emotions, meaning...) and feeling that it's too much sometimes.
- Deep meaning to live (ONS-UK, 2012)
- Parenting demands are non-negotiable and continuous (unlike work, one cannot resign from parenthood)
- Parenting involves emotional labor
- ...

**History of research**
*Contrast with previous self* is a dimension added later, identified in interviews. The most theoretically important addition (I'm not proud of who I am as a parent, I'm ashamed of the parent that I've become, I don't think I am a good father...)
- This dimension distinguishes **parents who are exhausted and changed** from **parents who have always been dismissive or disengaged**.

Any period more taxing than another? Not many longitudinal studies. Some cross-sectional evidence:
- 0-6 months: high demands/parental workload and parents still inexperienced, learning to be parents.
- But adolescence crucial period as well.

## The Balance between Risks and resources (BR2) theory

BR2 model: Parental burnout develops when parental resources are **chronically** insufficient to meet parenting demands (Mikolajczak & Roskam, 2018).

Resources --------- Demand/Risks
	        ▲

Demands / Risks factors:
- Parental perfectionism
- Low emotional intelligence
- Poor childrearing practices (parenting style, knowing concretely what to do, how to take care of the child... knowledge of parental practices)
- Excessive parental duties and chores
- Lack of co-parent support
- Absence of external support
- High parental standards

Resources / protection factors:
- Parental self-compassion
- High-emotional intelligence
- Good childrearing practices
- Time fo leisure and rest
- Positive coparenting relationship
- External social support
- Adequate financial resources

> [!NOTE]
> Absence of a risk =/ presence of the equivalent resource

## Elena's clinical case

"I no longer recognize the mother I have become"
38 years old high-school teacher, working full time.
Two children: Ginevra 7 and Samuel 3.
Goes to GP for general fatigue, insomnia.

Feels dread in the morning (not fatigue), "I cannot do this again today".
Escape ideation (not suicidal here - wants to disappear for a week)

BR2 analysis:
- **Risk contributors** (high parenting standards and perfectionism, absence of perceived partner support, excessive parenting load an time scarcity, expressive emotional suppression, internalized intensive parenting ideology...)
- **Resources**: vocational competence, friendship network (available but infrequently activated), cognitive reappraisal capacity (present but underutilized) (these three can be goals of intervention)

## Antecedents: etiology, risk factors, and vulnerabilities

Antecedents that are common for job and parental burnout, and specific antecedents to only parental.

- Specific: high parental standards, poor parenting, poor coparenting, disagreements, parental load
- **Psychological risk factors**: **neuroticism** (causes being more reactive to parenting stressors and are less able to regulate negative affect), **perfectionism**, **expressive suppression** (attempting to conceal or inhibit expression), **rumination** (abstract, repetititive, abstract thinking about negative states).
- Parenting-specific and family risk factors:
	- coparenting disagreement
	- family disorganisation: noise, crowding, home disorder (CHAOS scale)
	- parental load: high demands, and insufficient time
	- child with special needs
	- young children (<5yo) in the home
- Sociodemographic risk factors:
	- Gender (but it's mainly being the primary caregiver, not gender itself)
	- Single-parenthood
	- Part-time work (!): more time with child and less likely your work is fulfilling
	- More children: modest positive association (but some studies show that it can protect)
	- Not associated:
		- Parent's age (negligeable correlation)
		- Educational level
		- Single parenthood by choice (same as partnered parents in this case
		- Family type (?)

Other cultures: China: prevalence of model where the mother is the main coparent and grandmother secondary coparent, thus conflict/intergenerational trauma between the two are the most important predictors of parenting burnout there. So important in many cases to observe the larger family system.

**Intensive parenting norms**

Societal risk factors.
"Cultural ideology" that prescribes child-centered, selfless, emotionally demanding parenting, especially for mothers. Mikolajczak & Roskam 2022 "the cult of the child".

## Consequences

Individually:
- Escape ideation
- Suicidal ideation
- Addictive behaviors
- Sleep disorders
- Health deterioration

For the couple:
- Couple conflicts

For the children:
- 30% of variance in child behavior problems explained by parental burnout

## Marco's clinical case

Single father by circumstance. 46 yo. 3yo child mild intellectual disability.
"I used to be many things. Now I am only his father". 
Refered by neuropsychiatrist. "This is not a mental health issue. That is just a situation".

Single fathers: less likely to recognize and seek help for parental burnout.

His case:
- Separation, isolation, and social attrition.
- 45 min per day not allocated to the child or paperwork.
- Contrast with the previous parental self: "I just never have the energy {to bring him to the railway musem}. ANd when I think about going, I feel nothing. No anticipation, just the logistics of it". "Before all this, I was many things. Now I am only his father. And some days I am not even sure I am that properly".
- Emotional distancing: "He will bring me a model train he has assembled. And I will look a it and say the right things. But I am not there. I am running the protocol, not being his father." 
- Ambiguous loss: "I grieve for him sometimes. I know what a ten-year old boys life could be and his life is not that. And then I feel guilty for grieving. And then I feel guilty for feeling guilty".
